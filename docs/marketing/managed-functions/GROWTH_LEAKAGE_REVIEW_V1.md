# Growth Leakage Review v1

**Status:** Commercial offer definition / diagnostic. Docs only.
**Audience:** Owner-led SME / senior operator.
**Stage:** Consideration / conversion.
**Primary CTA:** Request a Growth Leakage Review.
**Commercial purpose:** Identify the smallest credible managed-growth problem worth fixing first.
**No public price is authorized by this document.**

## 1. Buyer-facing offer

### Headline

**Where is your business losing growth that already exists?**

### Subhead

CorpFlowAI reviews how opportunities are found, captured, followed up, retained, expanded and recovered — then identifies the smallest managed function worth fixing first.

### What the review covers

The review examines eight stages:

**Find → Attract → Capture → Nurture → Convert → Retain → Expand → Recover**

The buyer does not need to have sophisticated systems. The review can begin from the tools and records already in use.

### What the buyer receives

A concise diagnostic showing:
- the most credible growth leaks found;
- where the process currently breaks or depends on memory;
- what evidence supports the finding;
- what should remain human-controlled;
- the smallest managed-function intervention worth considering;
- what should **not** be built or changed yet;
- the next commercial decision.

### Trust line

**This is not a promise of guaranteed revenue and not a pretext for selling a large platform. The purpose is to find a real operating leak and decide whether fixing it is commercially worthwhile.**

## 2. Diagnostic question set

### A. Find — do you know who you want?

1. Which customer types are most valuable or most suitable today?
2. Are there target sectors, geographies or account types the business wants more of?
3. How are prospects currently identified?
4. Is prospect research systematic or ad hoc?
5. Are there dormant prospects or historical enquiries worth revisiting?
6. Are trigger events or buying signals monitored in any way?

**Leak signals:** no target definition; large unworked prospect lists; no owner; dormant opportunities never revisited.

### B. Attract — are the right people finding you?

1. Which channels currently create enquiries?
2. What content is produced consistently?
3. Which channels are paid vs organic?
4. Is paid advertising linked to a clear offer and follow-up process?
5. Can the business identify which campaigns or content create useful enquiries?
6. Is the website clear about the offer and next action?
7. Is the business discoverable in search and AI-assisted search for the problems it solves?

**Leak signals:** channel activity with no commercial goal; advertising before follow-up discipline; weak CTA; inconsistent content; no useful measurement.

### C. Capture — can every useful enquiry be seen?

1. Where can enquiries arrive?
2. Which inboxes, forms, numbers or platforms must someone monitor?
3. Is each qualifying enquiry recorded in one visible operating view?
4. Are missed calls/messages captured for follow-up?
5. Can management see unresolved enquiries today?

**Leak signals:** multiple uncontrolled inboxes; no shared status; messages depend on individual memory; no unresolved-work view.

### D. Nurture — what happens when the prospect is not ready now?

1. What happens to “not yet” prospects?
2. Is there a follow-up cadence?
3. Are useful content or reminders available for different prospect states?
4. Does anyone own deferred opportunities?
5. Are follow-ups appropriate to the customer's consent and channel expectations?

**Leak signals:** no next date; generic repeated messages; deferred leads disappear; uncontrolled outreach.

### E. Convert — what happens between interest and decision?

1. How are meetings/bookings arranged?
2. How are quotes/proposals issued and tracked?
3. How often are open quotes reviewed?
4. Who follows up objections or unanswered proposals?
5. Which decisions require owner approval?
6. Can the business distinguish customer delay from internal delay?

**Leak signals:** stale quotes; no next action; owner becomes routing bottleneck; no escalation rules.

### F. Retain — what happens after the first sale?

1. Is there a defined onboarding/follow-up process?
2. Are renewals or service intervals tracked?
3. Are satisfaction issues surfaced before a customer quietly leaves?
4. Are reviews/referrals requested where appropriate?
5. Are lapsed customers identified?

**Leak signals:** relationship disappears after invoice; renewals discovered late; churn invisible; no re-engagement process.

### G. Expand — do you systematically review existing customers for relevant additional value?

1. What other products/services can legitimately benefit existing customers?
2. Can the business identify customers using A but not relevant B?
3. Are renewal/upgrade windows visible?
4. Are normal repurchase patterns known?
5. Who decides whether an expansion approach is appropriate?
6. Is cross-sell/upsell tracked separately from indiscriminate marketing?

**Leak signals:** no customer segmentation; relevant opportunities left entirely to chance; aggressive generic upsell; no authority rules.

### H. Recover — what commercial work is already stranded?

1. How many qualifying enquiries are unresolved?
2. How many open quotes/proposals have no next action?
3. How many cancelled/no-show appointments have no recovery attempt?
4. How many dormant prospects were once genuinely interested?
5. Which customers have gone quiet relative to normal behaviour?
6. Which follow-ups are overdue?

**Leak signals:** unresolved work has no owner; no aging view; old opportunities are ignored because nobody knows where to start.

## 3. Evidence requested

The review should use the smallest evidence set necessary. Examples:
- enquiry/export list;
- CRM pipeline export;
- recent quotations;
- booking/calendar data;
- customer list with non-sensitive commercial fields;
- campaign summary;
- website/public channel review;
- existing follow-up process/SOP.

Do not request unnecessary personal/client-sensitive data. Prefer aggregate, redacted, synthetic or metadata-level evidence where enough to diagnose the process.

## 4. Scoring method

Each stage is rated:

- **0 — controlled:** clear owner, process and evidence;
- **1 — minor leak:** inconsistent but visible;
- **2 — material leak:** repeated failure or no dependable next action;
- **3 — critical leak:** high-frequency/high-value work routinely disappears or depends entirely on memory.

Add three modifiers:
- **Commercial value:** Low / Medium / High
- **Ease of intervention:** Easy / Moderate / Hard
- **Risk/regulatory exposure:** Low / Medium / High

Do not total these into a fake scientific score. Use them to prioritize judgment.

## 6. Example — illustrative only

**Largest credible leak:** Open quotations are issued but there is no systematic follow-up or aging review.

**Evidence:** Illustrative review finds 14 open quotes older than seven days; 9 have no recorded next action.

**Smallest safe intervention:** Managed Quote Follow-Up.

**What CorpFlowAI operates:** daily open-quote review, approved reminder/follow-up actions, status capture, escalation of pricing/scope objections, weekly summary.

**What remains human-controlled:** discounts, contract commitments, scope changes, sensitive negotiations.

**What not to build yet:** new CRM, new quoting system, bespoke dashboard.

This example is hypothetical and must be labelled illustrative on any buyer-facing surface.

## 8. Objection handling

### “Is this just a marketing audit?”

No. A conventional audit often stops at channels, traffic and campaigns. This review follows the commercial operating chain through capture, follow-up, retention, expansion and recovery.

### “Will you recommend a new CRM?”

Only if a real operating requirement cannot reasonably be met with what already exists. The default is to use the client's useful systems, not replace them.

### “Are you promising more revenue?”

No. The review identifies operating leakage and evidence. Any later service is accountable for the agreed function and service level, not for commercial outcomes outside its control.

### “Do you need all our customer data?”

No. The review should start with the minimum evidence necessary and avoid unnecessary sensitive information.
